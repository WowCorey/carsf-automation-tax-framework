Project

What CARSF Is

CARSF stands for Commonwealth Automation Revenue Stabilisation Framework. It is a private research/prototype policy and modelling framework for examining whether automation-heavy production can create labour-tax-base risk and how that risk could be made visible for review.

The project is structured as code, data manifests, report runners, generated reports, dashboards, guardrails, and reviewer packs. Its role is to make assumptions inspectable. It is not a replacement for restricted data access, public finance analysis, legal drafting, tax administration review, or statistical validation.

Core non-claim boundary

CARSF is not law, not legal advice, not tax advice, not ATO guidance, not Treasury modelling, not PBO costing, not official policy, not calibrated modelling, not validated modelling, not an actual tax payable calculation, not a firm-level liability determination, not economic validation, not welfare validation, not statistical validation, and not implementation readiness.

Policy question

What Problem It Tests

Labour share risk

Automation may reduce labour share in some sectors if productive work shifts toward capital, software, robotics, AI systems, or automation-heavy business structures.

Revenue-base visibility

Australia relies heavily on labour and income flows. CARSF tests whether a structured prototype can surface revenue-base risk without pretending the risk is already proven.

Review before claims

The repo separates public aggregate anchors, placeholders, boundaries, constraints, missing data, and review dependencies so reviewers can see what evidence is present and what remains blocked.

What Data Is Still Missing?

These categories are not loaded by the repo. If obtained through authorised channels, they would still require legal, tax, economic, statistical, privacy, and data-governance review before stronger claims could even be considered.

Restricted tax data

Needed to examine taxpayer-level or firm-level fiscal behaviour. Not loaded because it is restricted and not safe for the repo.

Firm-level confidential data

Needed for firm-specific liability or automation exposure review. Not loaded because it is confidential and outside repo scope.

Payroll and labour-cost data

Needed to move beyond wage anchors. It would not automatically prove CARSF liability or labour substitution.

Household microdata

Needed for distributional and welfare review. ABS DataLab, HILDA, DSS, and Services Australia records are not loaded.

Behavioural elasticity evidence

Needed before behavioural response claims. It would still require economic and statistical review.

Sector-specific automation exposure data

Needed for sector incidence review. Public aggregates alone do not establish causal exposure.

Legal and tax review

Needed to assess drafting, authority, administration, and tax-law boundaries. The repo does not provide advice.

Economic, statistical, and public finance review

Needed to test incidence, uncertainty, fiscal sufficiency, and methodology. The repo does not validate these claims.
