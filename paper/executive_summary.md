# Executive Summary

CARSF is a proposed framework for measuring and responding to automation-driven labour-tax-base risk in Australia.

The policy concern is not AI adoption itself. The concern is that automated productive capacity may shift fiscal capacity from taxable labour income to capital returns, while the Commonwealth revenue base remains heavily dependent on personal income tax and wage-linked activity.

The V1.4 architecture uses:

- Qualified Labour Contribution (QLC) to measure Australian labour contribution.
- Labour-Intensity Benchmark Cohorts (LIBCs) to anchor sector benchmarks.
- Human Labour Equivalent (HLE) to estimate benchmark labour needed for output.
- Automation Intensity Index (AII) to attribute the automated share of output.
- Net Labour Tax Gap (NLTG) to measure non-negative labour-tax-base exposure.
- Australian Automated Value Added (AAVA) to provide a capacity-to-pay base.
- Automation Equilibrium Levy (AEL), Automation Rent Levy (ARL), credits, and caps.
- CARS-I and CoverageRatio for national fiscal monitoring.

V1.5 development focuses on prototype sector schedules, a measurement appendix, QLC cap design, AAVA deductibility, open-source AI treatment, and R&D Tax Incentive interactions.
