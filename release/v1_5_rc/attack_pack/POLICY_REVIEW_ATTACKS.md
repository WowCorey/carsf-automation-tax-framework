# Policy Review Attacks

This attack document does not mean external review has been completed, does not mean approval has been granted, and does not mean validation has occurred. It is not legal advice, not tax advice, not ATO guidance, not Treasury modelling, not economic validation, not welfare validation, not statistical validation, not compliance scoring, not enforcement, and does not modify firm-level CARSF liability.

## What To Inspect First

- `reports/v1_5_release_candidate_pack.md`
- `reports/executive_dashboard.md`
- `release/v1_5_rc/REVIEWER_BRIEFING.md`

## Attack Questions

- Could public-facing language make the prototype sound like final policy design?
- Are calibration blockers visible before outputs?
- Does the release pack overstate coherence?
- Are equity, transition, social licence, and business-impact gaps prominent?
- Could any summary be quoted without its non-claim boundary?

## Likely Failure Modes

- Non-claim warnings are less visible than outputs.
- Release language sounds settled rather than critique-ready.
- Transition and equity questions are separated from fiscal or payment outputs.
- Review routing sounds formal rather than suggested.
- Missing blocker links make reports look more complete than they are.

## Required Evidence / External Review

- Policy review memo.
- Stakeholder impact challenge.
- Transition and equity review.
- Business impact review.
- Hostile-reader language review.

## What Not To Infer

- Do not infer approval, validation, readiness, final policy design, government endorsement, or firm-level liability change.

## Locked-Until-Review Items

- Public-facing claim language.
- Transition policy feasibility.
- Equity framing.
- Stakeholder acceptability.
- Business impact assumptions.

## Suggested Reviewer Output Format

Use a table with challenged language, affected file, overread risk, missing evidence, required external review, and proposed wording change.
