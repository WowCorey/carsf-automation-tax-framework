# Fiscal Trajectory Engine

Status: V1.5 prototype national-level modelling layer.

## Purpose

The fiscal trajectory engine tests the original fiscal problem behind CARSF: automation may reduce labour-linked public revenue while increasing transition and support-payment pressure.

It tracks placeholder PAYG erosion, superannuation contribution loss, HELP/HECS repayment loss, GST consumption effects, state payroll-tax pressure, support-payment pressure, automation revenue captured, and residual public-sector gaps.

## What Exists

- `model/carsf/workforce_displacement.py` for deterministic placeholder labour displacement and reabsorption.
- `model/carsf/public_revenue.py` for placeholder public-revenue impacts.
- `model/carsf/transfer_pressure.py` for placeholder support and retraining pressure.
- `model/carsf/fiscal_trajectory.py` for annual national fiscal trajectory outputs.
- `model/carsf/fiscal_sensitivity.py` for deterministic placeholder sensitivity sweeps.
- Fiscal examples under `examples/fiscal_trajectory/`.
- Reports generated by `python scripts/run_fiscal_trajectory.py`.
- Streamlit page `simulator/pages/13_Fiscal_Trajectory.py`.

## Run


Generated reports:

## Non-Claims

These are prototype fiscal trajectory outputs only. They are not forecasts, Treasury modelling, ATO estimates, ABS analysis, DSS estimates, PBO costing, legal advice, tax advice, or economic validation.

All fiscal values are illustrative placeholders. No real calibration values have been added. The national trajectory layer does not modify firm-level CARSF liability.

## Future Calibration Needs

Real calibration would require Treasury, ATO, ABS, DSS, PBO, state payroll-tax, superannuation, HELP/HECS, labour-market, transfer-payment, and industry datasets, plus legal and economic review.

Future Build 11 should add a UBI / transition-payment funding module only as a separate non-operative prototype layer.
